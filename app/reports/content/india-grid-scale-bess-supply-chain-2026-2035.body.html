The Thesis

India can procure and integrate grid-scale battery storage faster than it can build a competitive domestic cell industry. The central industrial question is how much value, technical learning, and supply security local firms can capture in integration, power electronics, and lifecycle services while domestic electrochemical production scales. Deployment is scaling, but execution and domestic cell manufacturing remain separate bottlenecks.

As of 30 June 2026, the Ministry of Power reported an aggregate national pipeline of 46.69 GW / 148.53 GWh across tendering, awarded, and under-construction stages. Against this, the Central Electricity Authority (CEA) establishes a structural 2031–32 planning requirement of 47.24 GW / 236.22 GWh for BESS. Under the ₹18,100 crore ACC PLI programme, 40 GWh has been awarded, but reported installed nameplate plant capacity stood at 1.4 GWh in July 2026. This report maps the supply chain, evaluates domestic value capture, and presents a sequenced decision agenda for 2026–2035.

The Framework

Techadyant Labs utilizes the Storage Sovereignty Index (SSI) and Storage Value Capture Ratio (SVCR) to measure domestic value added retained across ten supply-chain layers, net of imported intermediates. The Pack-to-Cell Gap Ratio (PCGR) tracks domestic operational pack nameplate against operational cell nameplate to quantify electrochemical dependence without conflating announcements with qualified output.

What It Means

For Policymakers

Publish consistent tender and commissioning data; tie PLI and VGF support to auditable factory milestones, eligible output, and value addition while preserving technology flexibility.

For Industry

Prioritize yield, qualification, and serviceability. Separate nameplate capacity from commercial output and target near-term opportunities in BMS, thermal management, and testing.

For Investors

Use milestone-weighted pipelines and downside cash flows. Treat uncontracted merchant revenues and announced GWh as risk until plant-level qualification is verified.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much battery storage does India need by 2031–32?
CEA estimates 47.24 GW and 236.22 GWh of BESS in the 2031–32 base case. Total storage is 73.93 GW and 411.4 GWh when pumped storage is included.
What is the ACC PLI scheme outlay and target?
The Advanced Chemistry Cell PLI programme has an ₹18,100 crore outlay and a 50 GWh target. MHI reported 40 GWh awarded in February 2025 across four beneficiary consortia.
How much BESS capacity is under construction?
The Ministry of Power reported 15,754 MW and 42,530 MWh under construction as of 30 June 2026. These represent sequential project development stages rather than operating grid assets.
Is battery storage mandatory for all renewable projects from July 2027?
This review found no operative national notification establishing a blanket July 2027 requirement. CEA issued a co-location advisory, and the ESO follows a separate trajectory.
What is the illustrative turnkey system cost used in the model?
The workbook uses ₹32,000/kWh as an illustrative turnkey system cost for a 100 MW/400 MWh example, producing a nominal capex of ₹1,280 crore.
