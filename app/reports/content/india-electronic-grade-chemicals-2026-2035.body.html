The Thesis

India's recurring demand for electronic-grade process chemicals, gases and related consumables rises from USD 86 million in 2026 to USD 264 million by 2035 on the base case. Contrary to the public narrative, wafer fabs account for fewer than one dollar in five of that demand (18%); solar cell lines (63%) and packaging plants (19%) are the commercially credible, near-term customers for Indian producers. Import dependence is 57% in 2026 and falls to 45% by 2035 on a location basis, but counting only molecules made in India it is still 62%. The most viable entries are HF/DHF/BOE upgrades and specialty-gas filling; photoresists and precursors are licensing plays, not synthesis plays.

Key Findings

Market reality vs public narrative

Wafer fabs buy USD 46 million of 2035 demand, fewer than one dollar in five. Solar cell lines buy USD 167 million and packaging plants USD 51 million.

Chapter 5 · base-case demand model [M]

How domestic is domestic?

Headline import dependence falls to 45% by 2035. Excluding on-site bulk gases it is 52%; counting only molecules made in India it is 62%, and 71% for tradable consumables.

Chapter 8 · depth-of-transformation classification [M]

The qualification barrier

The binding constraint is customer qualification and its infrastructure - certified packaging, trace-metal analytics, chain of custody - not plant capital. Semicon 2.0 funds capex; we found no instrument that pays for sampling, audits or split-lot trials.

Chapters 4.2 and 12 · policy review [V]

India's one commercial foothold

Among recurring process chemicals, Tanfac is the only producer found with an electronic-adjacent product in commercial supply: 10,000 TPA solar-grade DHF under two contracts worth about ₹1,068 crore to FY2028-29.

Chapter 4.3 · BSE filings [V]

An unresolved anchor

Tanfac's contracts (~USD 32 m a year) are 1.6x the modelled Indian solar HF pool. Either solar demand is understated or part of the volume is not Indian solar use; the filings do not say which.

Section 5.5 · contract-to-consumption bridge [M]

What not to chase

Photoresists (USD 9 m), CMP slurries (USD 4 m) and ALD/CVD precursors (USD 2 m) are strategically sensitive but too small for standalone domestic synthesis by 2035.

The Framework

Depth of transformation (T1-T3). Domestic supply is classified by how much is made here. T1: the molecule is made, or purified from Indian feedstock, in India. T2: imported feedstock is purified or formulated in India. T3: an imported finished product is filled, blended or repacked in India. The headline counts all three as domestic; the molecular measure counts only T1.

Readiness stages (S0-S4). Product families are staged from S0 (no domestic production identified) to S4 (qualified semiconductor-fab supply disclosed). No family has reached S4. Bulk gases sit at S2-S3 (contracted to the fab, pre-operation); HF/DHF/BOE is the most advanced liquid chemical at S2 (solar-grade commercial).

What It Means

For policymakers

The 30% capital support under Semicon 2.0 is necessary but not sufficient. If localisation follows the constrained path, 2035 domestic supply is USD 33 million lower (risk case R5), and about three-fifths of that loss sits in solar-grade supply. A qualification-cost window - a capped pilot reimbursing sampling, audits and third-party analytics, paid jointly to supplier and customer against completed gates - and eight- or ten-digit tariff lines for electronic grades are the two highest-leverage fixes.

For industry

Target solar and packaging plants first, not fabs. A solar or packaging reference strengthens a later fab evaluation, and Dholera's supplier window runs roughly 2028-2030. Bulk gases are already contracted to Linde India and INOX Air Products; new entrants should look at HF/DHF/BOE upgrades, packaging-plant consumables, or packaged specialty-gas filling and certified analytics - noting that filling improves service but does not remove molecular import dependence.

For investors

Underwrite on evidence of stage, not capacity announcements. A disclosed contract, a packaging and analytics line or a customer sample report is worth more than a headline capacity figure. Revenue arrives in steps, and early revenue is more likely to come from solar and packaging customers than from fabs.

Analytical Figures

The composition of demand reshapes the go-to-market for domestic producers. Packaging plants commission earlier than the front-end fab, and India's solar cell build-out is large; the single mature-node fab under construction reaches volume only from 2028.

Import dependence depends on what counts as domestic. The headline counts where the last production step happens, so a locally filled cylinder of imported gas counts the same as an Indian-made molecule. Stricter measures move the 2035 figure from 45% to as high as 71%.

Entry routes depend on size and readiness together. The largest pool, bulk gases, is held by incumbents; resists, CMP slurries and precursors are too small for greenfield synthesis. The opportunity sits in upgrading existing capability - HF/DHF/BOE, packaging-plant consumables and specialty-gas filling.

What to Watch

  • 2026-2027A qualification-cost window. Whether MeitY/ISM makes sampling, audits and split-lot trials fundable alongside the 30% capex support under Semicon 2.0.
  • 2027-2029Accredited analytics. Whether national or private laboratories obtain NABL-accredited scope for SEMI-type trace-metal methods, or certificates keep depending on foreign laboratories.
  • 2028-2030Dholera supplier evaluation. The Tata-PSMC fab evaluates suppliers during its ramp; candidates with a solar or packaging reference will be better placed.
  • OngoingTanfac's end customers. Disclosure of delivery schedules or end customers would settle whether solar demand and domestic supply are understated.
  • 2026-2028Multinational local filling. Announcements of local toll-blending or packaging by global suppliers would raise the headline domestic share without moving the molecular measure.

Frequently Asked Questions

How large is India's electronic-grade chemicals market by 2035?
Base-case recurring demand reaches USD 264 million in 2035, within a scenario range of USD 154-396 million, up from USD 86 million in 2026 - about 13% a year. Moving the consumption intensities to their bounds gives USD 181-381 million.
When does India's first wafer fab start volume production?
The Tata-PSMC fab at Dholera is expected to produce its first wafer and begin commercial operations in 2028 (mid-2028), starting at 55/90nm. The earlier December-2026 target dates from March 2024.
How import-dependent is India for electronic chemicals?
About 57% of 2026 demand is imported, falling to 45% by 2035 on the base case. Excluding on-site bulk gases the 2035 figure is 52%; counting only molecules made in India it is 62%.
Where can Indian firms enter first?
HF/DHF/BOE upgrades (USD 18 million contestable in 2035), specialty-gas filling (USD 30 million) and packaging-plant consumables (USD 18 million). None requires a fab qualification first.
Does Semicon 2.0 fund customer qualification?
Semicon 2.0 makes materials, chemicals and gases eligible for 30% capital support. We found no instrument that pays for sampling, supplier audits, split-lot trials or analytical certification.
