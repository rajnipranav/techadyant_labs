The Thesis

India has the rare-earth resource but not the magnet. It mined roughly 2,900 tonnes of REO in 2025 (about 0.7% of world output) and sits on a 13.15 Mt monazite resource — yet it operates no commercial-scale sintered NdFeB line, and the Ministry of Heavy Industries confirmed in July 2026 that current sintered-magnet demand is imported. China holds 60% of mined magnet rare earths, 91% of refined output and 94% of sintered magnet production. The gap is not in the ground; it is in the midstream — separation, metal, alloy and magnet-making — and in qualification.

This report maps the full rare-earth permanent-magnet value chain, sizes Indian demand to 2035 with a bottoms-up model, scores fifteen opportunity surfaces across the stack, and turns the map into a sequenced investment and policy agenda against the ₹7,280 crore REPM scheme and the four announced Rare Earth Corridors. Its central, counter-intuitive finding: the magnet line is not where India should move first.

Key Findings

The enablers out-rank the flagship

The highest-scoring surface in the stack is not the magnet line — it is a NABL magnet testing & qualification lab (4.20/5), at ₹40–120 cr of capex. Every REPM beneficiary and every OEM qualification cycle runs through a test house India does not possess.

Qualification is the silent killer

Magnet-making is "20% chemistry and 80% statistical process control." Without a domestic test house and OEM-anchored offtakes, India can commission lines that never ship a qualified part — GM's July 2026 termination of its e-VAC agreement is the cautionary tale.

Importing Chinese oxide just moves the dependency upstream

REPM lines fed on imported Chinese oxide reproduce the chokepoint one stage up. NdPr-oxide separation from monazite (4.05/5) is the surface that converts a verified resource into sovereign feedstock.

Demand nearly triples — even in the Bear case it grows

What to Watch

  • FeedstockThe strangle risk. REPM lines that import Chinese oxide reproduce the dependency upstream. The June 2025 suspension of IREL's 13-year Japan export arrangement shows Delhi understands the point; the 400-tonne separation base shows how far there is to go.
  • QualificationThe silent killer. Without a domestic test house and OEM-anchored offtakes, lines can be commissioned that never ship a qualified part (18–36-month cycles). GM's July 2026 e-VAC termination is the cautionary tale.
  • TechnologyTwo lanes that may not both hold. The Nexon–Giredmet pact routes through Rosatom just as the REPM scheme frames itself around "the allied group of nations." India may manage both lanes; it cannot assume both.
  • PriceShock-prone economics. NdPr ran US$55/kg (2024) to the high US$60s (2025), with reported spikes near US$119/kg FOB China in early 2026. A durable US–China truce could reset Chinese magnets to US$60–70/kg.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why can't India just build a magnet factory?
Because the magnet line is the end of a chain India has not built. Sintered NdFeB depends on NdPr-oxide separation, metal and alloy, and — critically — a qualification system. Magnet-making is "20% chemistry, 80% statistical process control," and without a domestic test house and OEM offtakes, a line can be commissioned that never ships a qualified part. This report ranks the testing lab, sub-components and separation above the magnet line itself.
How big is India's rare-earth magnet demand?
A bottoms-up model puts Base-case Indian NdFeB demand at roughly 7,740 tonnes/year by 2030 and 12,960 tonnes by 2035 (about US$604 million and US$1.01 billion at a modelled US$78/kg), nearly tripling over the decade. The Bull case reaches 18,200 t and the Bear case still grows to 7,800 t by 2035.
What is the REPM scheme?
The ₹7,280 crore Rare Earth Permanent Magnet scheme targets 6,000 tpa of integrated domestic capacity over seven years and attracted 20 bids by August 2026. It is the best-financed surface in the stack — but the report argues it only succeeds if the enabling surfaces (testing, sub-components, separation) are built alongside the magnet lines.
How dependent is the world on China for magnets?
China accounted for about 60% of mined magnet rare earths, 91% of refined output and 94% of sintered magnet production in 2024 (IEA). The concentration rises as you move downstream — which is exactly where India's gap sits.
Where should an investor or policymaker move first?
The three "build-now" surfaces are a NABL magnet testing & qualification lab (the single highest-scoring surface, ₹40–120 cr), magnet sub-components (SME-scale), and NdPr-oxide separation from monazite — with the flagship sintered line built alongside, not ahead of, them.
