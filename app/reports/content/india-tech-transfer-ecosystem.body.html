The Thesis

India's industrial technology transfer ecosystem is maturing but structurally concentrated. A 7.7% patent-to-spin-off conversion rate (142 from 1,850 filings) masks deep institutional asymmetry — IIT-Madras and IISc are over-indexed across all sectors, while DRDO dominates aerospace and materials but remains absent from IT and pharma. Programme density is high but impact variable, with IMPRINT and PMRF outperforming UAY and SPARC.

What It Means

For Policymakers

India's technology transfer ecosystem shows structural concentration risk. With IIT-Madras and IISc over-indexed across sectors, policy must diversify TLO capacity across the broader IIT system — IIT-Bombay, IIT-Delhi, IIT-Kanpur, and IIT-Hyderabad remain under-utilised relative to their research base. Programme density is high but impact variable; IMPRINT and PMRF demonstrate highest returns while UAY and SPARC require structural redesign. The 7.7% patent-to-spin-off conversion suggests room for clearer IP assignment rules and TLO professionalisation. For deeper institutional analysis, see our Signals coverage.

For Industry

Defence PSUs should prioritise engagement with DRDO for aerospace and materials, but look to IIT-Madras and IISc for IT and pharma-adjacent deep-tech. The 240+ project reviews indicate a maturing TRL pipeline, though the 1.1% DRDO cumulative proxy suggests conversion remains a bottleneck. Contract R&D programme selection should prioritise IMPRINT/PMRF tracks over UAY/SPARC. For dual-use applications, monitor MoD iPLEX disclosures for early-stage procurement signals. Institutional profiles are available in the Techadyant Atlas.

For Investors

The $400 billion stakes figure and 7.7% conversion rate suggest significant untapped value in patent-to-product pathways. Deep-tech venture formation remains concentrated in IIT-Madras/IISc spin-offs — investors should build origination networks across IIT-Bombay, IIT-Delhi, IIT-Kanpur, and IIT-Hyderabad to capture diversification alpha. Defence-adjacent startups face longer procurement cycles but higher strategic value; track MoD iPLEX disclosures for procurement pipeline visibility. Analytical Figures

The patent-to-spin-off conversion funnel is the single most revealing diagnostic of ecosystem health. Across 1,850 patent filings tracked by Techadyant Labs, only 142 have converted to registered spin-offs — a 7.7% rate that lags international benchmarks by a factor of 2–3x. The middle of the funnel (TRL assessment and licensing negotiation) is where the largest attrition occurs, suggesting TLO capacity constraints rather than invention quality as the binding constraint.

The 7.7% headline conversion rate masks significant institutional heterogeneity. IIT-Madras and IISc account for a disproportionate share of the 142 spin-offs, creating a concentration risk that leaves the broader ecosystem vulnerable to institutional-specific shocks. DRDO, despite dominating defence R&D expenditure, shows a 1.1% cumulative conversion proxy — an order of magnitude below the academic average. This asymmetry is the single most important structural feature of the ecosystem.

The sectoral presence matrix reveals a critical structural feature: DRDO's dominance is confined to aerospace and materials, with near-total absence from IT, pharma, and consumer-facing sectors. IIT-Madras and IISc, by contrast, show cross-sectoral reach but at the cost of depth in any single domain. This asymmetry shapes everything from procurement strategy to venture origination. For sectoral deep-dives on drones, space, semiconductors, and AI, see the Reports catalogue.
