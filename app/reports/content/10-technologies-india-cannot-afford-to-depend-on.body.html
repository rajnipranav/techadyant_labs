The Thesis

India's strategic technology challenge is not a list of imported products; it is the depth of the industrial stack beneath them. While policy targets and announced investments are strong signals, they are not outcomes. True resilience requires moving from pilot demonstrations to repeatable, customer-qualified industrial production. The most consequential gaps sit below the finished product—in process tools, materials, high-performance components, sensors, controls, software, and metrology.

The Framework

The Five Rungs of Capability

A country can occupy different rungs simultaneously within a single technology. Operating an imported system (Rung 1) and licensed manufacture (Rung 2) do not equate to component indigenisation (Rung 3), indigenous design authority (Rung 4), or owning the next generation (Rung 5). This report strictly separates target, award, commissioning, output, and qualification, refusing to let one stand in for another.

What It Means

For Policymakers

Shift focus from headline outlays to qualification dashboards. Fund shared industrial foundations—such as metrology, test facilities, and advanced materials—rather than running ten isolated self-reliance programmes. A rupee spent on a shared foundation pays dividends across multiple domains.

For Industry

The highest-return investments are in the unglamorous middle: advanced materials, precision machining, and process know-how. Secure anchor customers with multi-year commitments to survive order lumpiness and fund the qualification gap.

For Investors

Look beyond prototype announcements and ribbon-cuttings. Durable value is created in companies that bridge the "qualification gap" and achieve repeatable, customer-accepted commercial production with verified yield and uptime.

Analytical Figures

Public discourse often conflates policy announcements with operational reality. The scale of declared intent across four flagship programmes is substantial, totalling ₹1,58,884 crore. However, these outlays differ vastly in scope, duration, and disbursement status. They represent approved amounts and demand-side signals, not money spent or capacity operating. Understanding this disparity is critical to evaluating whether industrial capability is actually being built or merely promised.

The most acute measured concentration in this report lies in rare-earth permanent magnets. These small components carry outsized industrial consequences for electric vehicles, wind turbines, and defence equipment. While India possesses mineral resources and upstream separation capacity, the midstream capability to produce high-performance, reliable magnets at industrial scale is overwhelmingly imported. The data shows a clear and worsening concentration of supply from a single geography, turning a commercial relationship into a strategic point of leverage.

Similarly, the machine-tool industry presents a deceptive aggregate picture. While domestic production is substantial and growing, the high-end frontier remains imported. A machine tool is effectively a computer with a spindle; when a domestic builder imports its CNC controls, drives, and precision encoders, it imports a substantial share of the machine's value and differentiation. The aggregate import share of 59.4% masks an even deeper dependence in the highest-performance segments required for aerospace and semiconductor manufacturing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is India's import dependence on rare-earth permanent magnets?
In FY2024-25, China accounted for 81.3% of India's metallic permanent-magnet imports by value and 90.4% by quantity (HS 85051190). This represents a sharp increase from 70.9% by value in FY2022-23.
How much ACC battery capacity is actually commissioned in India?
As of 30 June 2026, only 1.4 GWh of cell capacity has been commissioned against a 50 GWh programme target. This represents 2.8% of the target and belongs to a single beneficiary.
What is the total outlay for India's semiconductor and quantum missions?
The Semicon 2.0 outlay is ₹1,27,500 crore (approved July 2026). The National Quantum Mission has an approved outlay of ₹6,003.65 crore for the period 2023-24 to 2030-31, funding four thematic hubs.
Does India have indigenous design authority for fighter aero engines?
Not established from public evidence. Current capability consists of licensed manufacture and overhaul (e.g., AL-31FP), while indigenous design (e.g., Kaveri) remains in the testing phase without full flight qualification data disclosed.
What percentage of machine tools does India import?
Imports represent approximately 59.4% of domestic consumption by value in FY2025-26. While domestic production is broad, high-end segments like five-axis machining and CNC controls show probable import dependence.
